# Document Writing Guidelines

This document contains only content and presentation guidance that is missing from the current `google-docs-report` skill. It is a proposed supplement, not a replacement for the skill's existing rules.

## Scope

- The user chooses the template. The AI prepares the report body in that template; the user fills in the template fields. Template selection and title-page data-entry instructions are therefore excluded.
- These recommendations apply to Google Docs report content and presentation. Guidance for Google Sheets and Google Slides is excluded.
- Existing skill requirements are not repeated here, including the report structure, a concise introduction and purpose, the Hubstaff reference, descriptive links instead of raw URLs, a glossary for specific terms, the methodology/results/analysis/visualization sections, recommendations and limitations in conclusions, Heading styles and numbering, real lists, consistent font and formatting, table and caption rules, and proofreading before delivery.

## Organizing the Narrative

- Move from the general to the specific: establish the problem and necessary context, then explain methods and details, followed by analysis, results, and conclusions.
- Order sections and subsections to make the reasoning easy to follow. Group related information; do not mix methodology, results, problem analysis, and technical details without a clear connection.
- Present processes or events chronologically when chronology is relevant.
- Use brief transitions to explain how each section relates to what came before and what follows.
- For explanatory documents, consider organizing the narrative around questions and answers: What happened? Why does it matter? How was it investigated or addressed? What do the results show? What should happen next?
- After a data-heavy section, summarize what the data means and how it relates to the report's purpose.

## Relevance, Completeness, and Level of Detail

- Include information that supports the report's purpose, helps readers understand the results, or is needed to make a decision. Exclude details that add neither useful context nor value to the conclusions.
- Match the level of detail to the audience. Technical readers may need methods and implementation details; executives or clients generally need the main point first. When a reader needs to assess a decision, explain the reasons for it and relevant trade-offs.
- Avoid overwhelming the reader with figures or step-by-step process detail that does not affect understanding or conclusions. Do not omit important stages, unsuccessful results, risks, or limitations merely because they are unfavorable or inconclusive.
- Check that significant stages and outcomes are covered, and that the same fact is not repeated in multiple places without a distinct purpose or interpretation.
- Describe risks and next steps at the level of detail needed to understand their consequences and make decisions.

## Clarity, Tone, and Terminology

- Use clear, concise business language. Keep the tone formal but not stiff; avoid lengthy constructions and words that add no meaning.
- State conclusions objectively and support them with evidence. Do not present an impression or assumption as a result; identify uncertainty explicitly.
- Use terms established by the company or relevant professional community. Refer to the same entity consistently throughout the document.
- Avoid unnecessary anglicisms when a well-established equivalent exists in the document's language. Avoid colloquialisms, slang, and abbreviations that the intended audience may not understand.
- For mixed or non-technical audiences, explain specialized terms and abbreviations at first use. Do not burden the explanation with technical detail that is unnecessary for comprehension.

## Additional Heading Rule

- Use the `Title` style only for the document title. Format section and subsection headings with the appropriate `Heading` styles, reflecting their position in the document hierarchy.

## Content Review Checklist

- Can readers understand the problem, context, and purpose without additional explanation?
- Does the narrative move from general context to specific details, and does the section order follow the logic or chronology of the process?
- Does each substantial passage support the report's purpose? Is the information sufficiently complete without unnecessary repetition?
- Is the level of detail appropriate for the audience? Are decision rationales, relevant risks, and unsuccessful results explained where needed?
- Do the conclusions follow from the evidence and connect the results to the report's purpose?
- Are terms used consistently, and are abbreviations understandable to the intended reader?
- Are complex passages divided into logical paragraphs, with informative subsection headings?
